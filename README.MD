I am a scientist working in drug development with nearly 10 years of experience in the field and for the past 6 years, I have been working on a CAR-NK cell therapy at a startup company named Senti Biosciences. Our main investigational product, SENTI-202 is a first-in-class, off-the-shelf CAR-NK cell therapy designed to selectively target CD33- and/or FLT3-expressing hematologic malignancies including relapsed/refractory acute myeloid leukemia (AML), while sparing endomucin-expressing healthy hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells even when they express CD33/FLT3. In this program, I initally worked on validating multiple drug candidates in-vivo assessing safety and efficacy with various mice models, characterized NK cell pharmocokinetics in mouse peripheral blood and tissues using flow cytometry and IHC, and eventually supported and executed IND-enabling studies for SENTI-202. To this end, I developed the tissue cross-reactivity study on frozen human tissue arrays to demonstrate key safety data of our lead candidate, optimized and executed cell-based killing assays of our lead candidate against multiple cancer target cell lines, and developed two 12-color flow panels for in-depth immunophenotyping of human peripheral blood to interrogate future patients' immune response in the Phase 1 clinical trial.
Over the course of my time at Senti Biosciences, I realized how valueable and efficient it was to develop and utilize data pipelines with R to automate workflows for data wrangling and data analysis. I applied these skills to the large data sets that were generated from the two immunophenotyping flow panels I ran, ddPCR PK data from patients, and cytometry by time-of-flight (CyTOF) data. With these skills, I was able to quickly generate bar graphs and heatmaps, compare different cell populations with statistical significance, and find interesting patient-to-patient response relationships. By this time, I already had an interest in bioinformatics and wanted to dive deeper into scRNAseq data and how it could potentially inform the selection process for NK donors that would result in engineered CAR-NK cells with desirable attributes such as increased cytotoxicty and adaptive-like phenotypes. As I transition out of wet-lab, this is where I will be sharing my journey. The learning-log repo is my first repo covering what I've been learning in the "Data Carpentry: Introduction to the Command Line for Genomics" course available online. It covers 5-10 small scripts with comments explaining what each does, and a conda environment.yml I created.
I am working to move from the bench to bioinformatics, where my aim is to apply my background of translational immunology and CAR-NK cell therapy + clinical data exposure towards a bioinformatics Masters degree and eventual PhD doctorate.
